## Findings and caveats (read before running a serious game)

- Volatility tiers separate cleanly (low < medium < high) and news-affected stocks move ~3x more than unaffected ones, so news matters more than noise. Round 12 is the most volatile, by design.
- **Budget gap dominates the winner rule.** Because the winner is the highest absolute wealth and the host may give players budgets up to 25% apart, while a typical game moves portfolios by single-digit percentages, the biggest budget usually wins. The rules are locked, so nothing was changed; the results screen therefore also shows a "Best return" award. For a fair race, give players equal or near-equal budgets.
- **Power-ups are not free money.** At the locked price (€5,000) these simple bots lose return when they buy one: Shield and Rocket paid far less than their cost on average, and the Oracle only gives information. They can pay off only with concentrated, well-timed positions (Rocket needs a large position in the chosen stock; Shield needs real exposure). No power-up decided games on its own.
- Patience (20% rate) matters only for positions held 4+ rounds and sold at a profit; tax drag on the active strategies is modest compared with market noise.
- Bots are deliberately simple; human skill will differ.
